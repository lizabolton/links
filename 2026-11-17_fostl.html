In their own words

There was a famous video on Chinese TikTok created by a Chinese English language learner (ELL). There was nothing beside a photo of her lecture room and a text in the left corner of the image, saying “Dear professor, you would feel that I am much smarter, kinder and funnier if I could speak my own language to you.”
Language is not merely a technical skill—it is part of culture, identity, and how students make their intelligence, kindness, and humour known to us.
A note on terminology: When I refer to multilingual learner, I am thinking about students who speak another language more fluently than they speak English AND need support to engage confidently in the standard English dialect of their educational environment.

Learning so far Insights from Talk Data to Me? (Bolton et al., 2026)

Language & cognitive load

Statistical learning bridges mathematical notation, academic prose, and lexically ambiguous terms (significance, random, model); a 3-second pause outperforms speaking slowly.

Small — Medium — Big scales

  • Small: Pause at natural boundaries; speak and write instructions; use shared languages for encouragement and English for technical terms.
  • Medium: Immerse students in datasets and data dictionaries before timed assessments; modified Think–Pair–Share with anonymous submission raised active participation in China from near-zero to 50–70%.
  • Big: Scaffolded low-stakes writing and TA language support helped 57% of initially lower-confidence ELLs reach Good/Excellent self-rated proficiency (vs 44% of peers, N = 1,697).
