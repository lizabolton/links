In their own words

There was a famous video on Chinese TikTok created by a Chinese English language learner (ELL). There was nothing beside a photo of her lecture room and a text in the left corner of the image, saying “Dear professor, you would feel that I am much smarter, kinder and funnier if I could speak my own language to you.”
Why this resonates in Statistics: Language is not merely a technical skill—it is part of culture, identity, and how students make their intelligence, humour, and humanity known to us.

Learning so far: Talk Data to Me?

1. Multi-layered language & lexical ambiguity

Statistics bridges mathematical notation, academic prose, and everyday words with distinct technical meanings (significance, random, model, variable, average) alongside hedging language.

2. Pausing > speaking slowly & multimodal prompts

Inserting 3-second pauses at grammatical boundaries supports the 4-step translation–thinking cycle far better than artificial slow speech, paired with spoken + written instructions and clear translanguaging norms.

3. Scaffolded immersion & classroom evidence

Pre-exam data immersion reduces timed cognitive load; modified Think–Pair–Share with anonymous writing lifted China classroom responses from ~0% to 50–70%, while a UofT survey (N = 1,697, 76–83% ELL) saw 57% of ELLs starting at Poor/Adequate writing reach Good+ by course end.
